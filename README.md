# Hi, I'm Harold 👋

I'm a Technical Program Manager at Microsoft working at the intersection of **Copilot, connected data, customers, and engineering**.

A lot of my work starts with some version of the same problem:

> **Something important is true, but the people, systems, and evidence needed to act on it aren't connected yet.**

That's the kind of problem I like.

I work with customers and engineering teams to understand what's actually happening, separate signal from noise, find the right technical or product path, and turn ambiguity into something people can act on.

## How I think

I'm naturally a systems thinker.

When I encounter a problem, I rarely stop at the immediate question. I want to understand the system around it:

```text
                    ┌───────────────┐
                    │    Observe    │
                    │ What is true? │
                    └───────┬───────┘
                            │
                            ▼
                 ┌─────────────────────┐
                 │     Understand      │
                 │ What system am I in?│
                 └──────────┬──────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
                  ┌───────────────────┐
                  │      Connect      │
                  │ What am I missing?│
                  └─────────┬─────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
                   ┌─────────────────┐
                   │      Judge      │
                   │ What matters?   │
                   └────────┬────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
                   ┌─────────────────┐
                   │       Act       │
                   │ What's next?    │
                   └────────┬────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
                 ┌─────────────────────┐
                 │       Capture       │
                 │ Can this be reused? │
                 └──────────┬──────────┘
                            │
                            └──────────────► back to Observe
```

That last step matters to me.

If I solve the same kind of problem twice, I start wondering whether the reasoning itself should become reusable.

Sometimes that becomes a prompt. Sometimes a script. Sometimes a skill, agent, plugin, workflow, or piece of documentation.

The artifact isn't the goal.

**Better leverage is.**

## What I work on

My professional focus is **Copilot and connected data**.

I'm especially interested in the problems that appear where customer needs meet product behavior:

I'm particularly drawn to the messy middle between **"the technology can do this"** and **"the customer is successfully doing this."**

There's usually a lot hiding inside that gap.

## How I use AI

I don't think of AI primarily as a chatbot.

I think of it as a new layer in the way I work with information, tools, and systems.

My preferred pattern looks something like:

```text
Human judgment
      ↓
Context + evidence
      ↓
AI reasoning
      ↓
Tools + systems
      ↓
Action
      ↓
Verification
      ↓
Human judgment
```

The human stays in the loop.

I'm interested in giving AI enough context and access to useful tools that it can do substantive work, while keeping judgment, verification, and consequential decisions grounded in evidence.

That has led me deep into **agents, skills, plugins, MCP, prompts, context engineering, tool orchestration, and AI-assisted workflows**.

## The tools change. The pattern doesn't.

Today I spend time with things like:

`Copilot` · `Copilot Studio` · `GitHub Copilot` · `GitHub` · `VS Code` · `MCP` · `Microsoft Graph` · `PowerShell` · `Python`

But I'm deliberately not defining myself by the toolchain.

The interesting problem is how the pieces connect.

A new model, API, agent framework, connector, or protocol is useful when it helps close the distance between **information and understanding, understanding and decision, or decision and action**.

## A principle I keep coming back to

> **Don't automate the ambiguity. Understand it first.**

Good automation starts with a good mental model.

So I tend to investigate before I abstract, verify before I automate, and keep humans involved where judgment matters.

Once the pattern becomes clear, though, I want the system to remember what we learned.

That's where things get interesting.
